01 / THE PROBLEM

From points to a term structure

A yield curve maps the maturity of a fixed-income instrument to its yield. Market data rarely provides a quote for every possible maturity, so a model can produce a continuous curve from a finite set of observations.

This study works with 15 tenors, from three months to thirty years. Each observation is a pair $(t_i, y_i)$, where $t_i$ is maturity in years and $y_i$ is the quoted market yield.

02 / THE MODEL

Nelson-Siegel-Svensson

The NSS model combines a long-run level, a slope factor, and two curvature factors:

\[ y(t) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 L_1(t; \tau_1) + \beta_2 L_2(t; \tau_1) + \beta_3 L_3(t; \tau_2) \]

The loading functions are:

\[ L_1(t; \tau_1) = \frac{1-e^{-t/\tau_1}}{t/\tau_1} \]
\[ L_2(t; \tau_1) = \frac{1-e^{-t/\tau_1}}{t/\tau_1} - e^{-t/\tau_1} \]
\[ L_3(t; \tau_2) = \frac{1-e^{-t/\tau_2}}{t/\tau_2} - e^{-t/\tau_2} \]
β0Long-run level
β1Short-term slope
β2, β3Curvature effects
τ1, τ2Decay constants

As \(t\) becomes large, the exponential terms approach zero and the curve approaches \(\beta_0\). This gives the model an interpretable long-end anchor while allowing more than one hump.

03 / THE FIT

Calibration by least squares

For each date, the optimizer starts with a six-parameter initial guess. At every candidate parameter vector, the model produces a fitted yield at each observed maturity. The difference from the market quote is the residual:

\[ e_i = y_i - \widehat{y}(t_i;p) \]

The objective is the sum of squared residuals:

\[ \mathrm{SSE}(p) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[y_i-\widehat{y}(t_i;p)\right]^2 \]

Squaring makes positive and negative errors contribute equally, while penalizing large misses more heavily. The selected parameters are the ones that minimize this total:

\[ \widehat{p} = \arg\min_p \mathrm{SSE}(p) \]

The NSS fit is nonlinear because the decay constants appear inside exponential functions. scipy.optimize.minimize searches the parameter space, evaluates the objective repeatedly, and returns the best solution it finds for each of the five dates.

IMPLEMENTATIONUnweighted nonlinear least squaresEvery quoted tenor contributes equally; production models may add weights, bounds, convergence checks, and sensitivity analysis.

04 / THE TOOLS

Interpolation and valuation

Model-based interpolation

Some maturities are not directly quoted. The model converts the requested tenor into years and evaluates the calibrated NSS curve at that point:

\[ \widehat{y}(t^{*}) = y(t^{*};\widehat{p}) \]

For example, 9M becomes \(t^{*}=0.75\) years. The project evaluates the curve at 0.75, 3.5, 12.5, and 25 years for each date. This is model-based interpolation because the result follows the smooth NSS shape rather than a straight line between two quotes.

The 25Y estimate lies between the observed 20Y and 30Y points. A maturity beyond 30Y would be extrapolation and should be treated with greater caution.

Day-count fractions

Day-count conventions turn calendar time into a numerical year fraction. For actual elapsed days:

\[ \mathrm{Act/360}=\frac{\mathrm{days}}{360},\qquad \mathrm{Act/365}=\frac{\mathrm{days}}{365} \]

For 548 days, the fractions are approximately 1.5222 and 1.5014. The denominator matters because it changes the time used for interest accrual and discounting.

Present value / annual compounding\[ PV=\frac{FV}{(1+r)^T} \]With FV = EUR 2,430.04, r = 5%, and T = 5 years: PV = 2,430.04 / (1.05)^5 ≈ EUR 1,904.00. A full bond valuation would discount each coupon and principal cash flow separately.

05 / SCOPE AND ASSUMPTIONS

Designed for clarity

This is a focused fixed-income analytics implementation, not a live market-data service. The input rates are hard-coded historical observations, the fit is unweighted, and no parameter bounds are imposed.

A production curve engine could extend this foundation with liquidity weights, constrained optimization, discount-factor fitting, instrument cash flows, and automated data ingestion. Stating these assumptions clearly keeps the analysis reproducible and decision-ready.
